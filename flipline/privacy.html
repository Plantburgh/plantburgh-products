Flipline Privacy Policy

Effective 2026-07-26 · revised 2026-11-22

Flipline is an offline-first reselling companion. This policy is short because the app is built not to collect your data.

What we collect

We (the developer) receive nothing about you. Flipline has no accounts, no sign-up, no analytics of our own, and no server that receives your data. The free version shows Google ads, and Google's ads software collects some data on its own (see Ads and your privacy choices).

Google Play counts anything an app sends off the device as "collected", even when it goes straight to a third party. By that definition Flipline's own features collect two things, only when you use the feature that needs them: photos you send for identification, and search terms (saved hunts, typed searches, barcodes, item titles and brands for a price or recall check). Both are described below. The ads software adds the data listed under Ads and your privacy choices.

Where your data lives

Everything you enter (inventory, purchases, sales, expenses, mileage, photos, API keys, AI spending totals, your profile) is stored on your device only. API keys and sign-ins sit in Android's encrypted key store, apart from your records. Backups exist only where you choose to share or save them, and in Android's own backup.

Android backup. Flipline keeps one copy of your records (not photos, not API keys, not sign-ins, not your Pro status) for Android's backup. Android saves it with your Google account only when it can encrypt it end to end, which needs a screen lock on your phone, and copies the same file when you move to a new phone by cable or Wi-Fi. A new phone or a reinstall gets your records back. Plantburgh never receives it, and nothing else in the app is backed up this way. Turn it off in your phone's Settings › Google › Backup.

Home-screen widgets

Flipline has five optional home-screen widgets: a Scan button, a Quick list (scan barcodes or a whole table onto a list), this week's profit, the best deal in your feed, and your top three deals. The app writes their numbers to a small file on your phone, and the widgets only read that file. They fetch nothing and send nothing. Anyone who can see your home screen can see what they show, so remove a widget if you would rather keep those numbers out of sight.

AI assistants

Flipline's assistant tools (ChatGPT, Claude and others). An assistant you connect to Flipline's tools at https://plantburgh-flipline.web.app/mcp can ask for marketplace fees, profit after fees, the most to pay, and a haggle plan. It sends only the numbers and choices for that one calculation, such as a price, a marketplace and a shipping cost, and gets the math back. The tools never see your items, sales or account, there is no sign-in, and we do not store what was asked. To keep the free tools fair, each request's IP address is turned into a one-way code and counted per day (up to 300 calculations a day); that count is deleted automatically after 3 days. The assistant handles your conversation under its own privacy policy.

Your phone's assistant (Pro, Android). On phones that support it, Gemini can ask Flipline what something is worth, the most to pay, your feed and your inventory. That happens on your phone between the two apps, and Flipline sends nothing to us. Saving anything opens Flipline for you to confirm. What Gemini does with an answer is under Google's terms. See Profile › AI assistants.

Photos never carry your location

Your phone's camera may tag each photo with where it was taken. Flipline removes those tags (location and the rest of the camera details) from every photo before it leaves your device: to an AI provider, to Google Lens, to eBay, or when you share a photo. The copy kept on your device is unchanged.

What leaves your device

Data leaves your device only when you use a feature that calls a third-party service, using keys or sign-ins you supplied where that service needs them. That includes the deal feed you set up: it refreshes when you open the app or tap refresh, and, if you turn on Alerts with Flipline closed (More), about once an hour in the background while you have a saved hunt.

  • Deal sources, price and recall lookups. Your search or refresh sends the query to that service:
    • Deal feeds: GSA Auctions (through api.data.gov), Best Buy, Walmart, CJ Affiliate, Woot, Meh and DealNews.
    • Feeds you add yourself: any public deal feed whose address you paste or pick from our list, for example DealNews categories, CheapShark and GamerPower (game deals and giveaways) and other deal sites' RSS feeds. The request goes to the site that publishes the feed.
    • Prices and product facts: Discogs, Scryfall (Magic cards), the Pokémon TCG API (pokemontcg.io), Open Library, Open Food Facts and UPCitemdb.
    • Safety: the U.S. CPSC recall database (saferproducts.gov).
    • Test builds given to a small group of testers can also read Reddit, Slickdeals and eBay searches. The Play Store version does not.
    Your home location is used on the device to filter results and is not sent.
  • Photo ID. You choose how a photo is identified:
    • On this phone: Gemini Nano where your phone supports it, and Google's on-device ML Kit readers (object finder, labels, text reader) on every other Android phone. The photo is read on the device and sent nowhere.
    • Google Lens: you hand the photo to Google's Lens app yourself, and Google handles it under its own terms. Flipline sends nothing.
    • A cloud AI you connected under your own API key and that provider's terms: Google Gemini, Anthropic Claude, OpenRouter, or any other AI service you add a key for (OpenAI, xAI Grok, Mistral, Groq, DeepSeek, Together, Fireworks, Perplexity, or any address you type for a service that speaks the OpenAI API). The photo goes only to the AI you pick, or the first one in the order you set in Money › Shipping & AI costs › AI providers & routing. In lot mode, one photo becomes several requests: the whole photo, then close-up crops of it. Photos are never sent automatically.
    • A model on your own computer (Ollama or LM Studio) that you add by its address on your Wi-Fi: the photo goes over your own network to that computer and nowhere else. Flipline sends it in plain http only to a private address (192.168…, 10…, 172.16–31…, 127…, localhost, or a name ending in .local) and refuses it for any other address. That traffic is not encrypted, so use it only on a network you trust.
    • AI off: Money › Shipping & AI costs › Use AI to identify photos off. No photo is sent to any AI.
    What each cloud provider does with a photo:
    • Gemini free tier: Google states it may use submitted content to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it. A paid Gemini key is not used that way.
    • Anthropic Claude: Anthropic does not train on API content by default.
    • OpenRouter passes the photo to the company that runs the model you reach. Flipline picks free models, and free models are often run by providers that log requests and may train on them. You can turn that off in your OpenRouter account's privacy settings.
    • Any other service you add: its own API terms and data policy apply. Read them before you add the key.
    • Picture match sends each item's crop to the AI that does that job (Gemini, or another you added that can search the web), the same way.
  • Posting a listing to eBay (optional, Pro, one tap per item, using your own eBay developer keys and eBay sign-in): the title, description, price and photos you chose go to eBay. eBay hosts the photos publicly. Offers you send to an item's watchers go to eBay the same way. Flipline keeps your eBay sign-in token in the device's secure storage and never sees your eBay password.
  • Links you tap (a marketplace search, a deal, directions for a pickup route) open in your browser or in that company's app, and that site handles the visit under its own policy.
  • Sharing: backups and CSV exports go wherever you share them.

Each third-party service processes those requests under its own privacy policy. Flipline adds no identifiers of its own to any request.

Public files the app downloads. To stay current without an update, the app downloads these public files. The requests carry nothing about you; like any website, the host sees your IP address.

  • From this website (hosted by GitHub Pages): a list of AI models (models.json), a table of AI prices (prices.json), ready-made deal feeds (feed_templates.json) and the Free and Pro plan settings (plans.json).
  • From our offers server (app.giganize.work/promos.json): the list of sponsored offers and of our other apps (More › More from Plantburgh), a few times a day.

Every request Flipline makes to the internet uses https, pictures shown with a deal included. The one exception is an AI model you run on your own computer (above), which stays on your own network.

Ads and your privacy choices

The Play Store version of Flipline shows banner ads served by Google AdMob. Google's Mobile Ads SDK starts when the first ad is due and, under Google's privacy policy, collects and shares with Google: your device's advertising ID and app set ID, ad interactions (views and taps), diagnostics and crash data from the SDK, and your approximate location from your IP address. Google uses this to show and measure ads and to prevent fraud.

  • Watched-ad unlock. If you choose to watch a rewarded ad for the day's extra comps, Flipline keeps the date you last used it on your device and in your Google account through Google Block Store, so the once-a-day limit survives a reinstall. Only that date is stored there; we never receive it.
  • Consent. Before the first ad, Flipline runs Google's consent tool (User Messaging Platform) and shows a consent form where your state's law requires one. Without consent, Google serves non-personalised ads.
  • Change your choices any time in More › Ads › Privacy choices (shown where a choice applies to you).
  • Advertising ID: reset or delete it in your phone's Settings › Privacy › Ads (or Settings › Google › Ads).
  • Flipline Pro turns banner ads off, and the ads SDK never starts for a Pro user.

Ads never see your inventory, prices, photos, or keys. Those stay on your device.

Sponsored offers and affiliate links

Flipline shows a few Sponsored offers of its own: deals from stores we partner with through CJ Affiliate, and cards for our own other apps and services, marked From Plantburgh. They appear beside the Feed title, now and then as a row between deals in the feed, and, when Google has no ad to show, in the banner space. They are plain links. The app sends nothing about you to show them; tapping one opens the page in your browser (a store's through CJ's tracking link), and CJ and the store may then set their own cookies under their own privacy policies.

Some links to marketplaces are affiliate links. Links to eBay carry our eBay Partner Network tag, so eBay may pay us a commission if you buy after following one, and eBay may set its own cookies under its own policy. When a link can earn us a commission, the app says so next to it. It costs you nothing extra and changes nothing about the price you pay.

Flipline Pro

If you buy Pro, Google Play handles the payment. Flipline never sees your card. The app keeps only that you have Pro, which plan, and when Google Play last confirmed it, on this device. Pro users see no banner ads and can hide sponsored offers.

Data retention and deletion

We keep nothing about you. The only thing on our servers is the daily count for Flipline's assistant tools described above: a one-way code of an IP address and a number, deleted automatically after 3 days. Data the ads SDK sent to Google is Google's; see its privacy policy. To erase all app data, use More › Account & settings › Erase all data in the app, or delete the app. Remove any Flipline widgets from your home screen too. Photos and searches already sent to an AI or search provider are that provider's to delete. To revoke an AI provider's access, disconnect it in the app and revoke the key with that provider.

Children

Flipline is a business tool for adults and is not directed at anyone under 18. Its photo identification runs on AI services that require users to be 18 or older.

Languages

The app runs in English, Spanish, Chinese (Simplified), Tagalog, Vietnamese and Arabic. It follows your phone's language, and you can pick one in More › Account & settings › Profile › Language. This policy is written in English. If a translation of it ever differs from the English, the English version governs.

Changes

If a future version ever changes what leaves the device, this policy and the in-app copy will be updated first, and the change will be opt-in.
