Replicost: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-25 · Plantburgh Products LLC, a Pennsylvania company owned by Plantburgh LLC

Last updated: 25 November 2026

Replicost is made by Plantburgh Products LLC, a Pennsylvania limited liability company.

This policy is short because the app is simple: we do not run a server, and we do not collect anything. There is no account to create, no analytics, and no tracking by us. The version installed from an app store shows ads from Google (see "Advertising" below). Everything the app computes, it computes on your device, and everything it remembers, it remembers on your device.

Data about you leaves your phone only when you start it: photo identification (described in full below), and a few public lookups you tap for yourself (see "Lookups you start"). The app also reads two small public files, plan settings and current prices (see "Plans and purchases" and "Price updates"), and talks to Google Play about purchases; none of these carries anything you typed, saved or photographed.

What stays on your device

All of it, unless you connect a Gemini key:

  • Saved estimates, projects, and their ledgers
  • Your shop profile — metro, state, hourly rate, tools, skills, municipality
  • Anything you have reported: actual costs, quotes you were given, notes on a line
  • Your Gemini API key, held in the device keychain
  • The daily identification counter

None of this is transmitted to us. We have nowhere to transmit it to.

Photo identification on your phone (no key)

On a phone that has Google's Gemini model built in (Gemini Nano, for example on recent Samsung Galaxy and Google Pixel phones), and when you have not connected a key, Replicost identifies the photo on the phone itself. The photo is not sent to Google or to us; it is read by the phone's own model and then discarded. On phones without that model, Replicost uses the phone's own offline image labeler instead, which also keeps the photo on the phone.

Photo identification with your own key sends your photo to Google

When you connect a Google Gemini API key and identify something by photograph, the photograph is sent to generativelanguage.googleapis.com using your own key, under your own Google account.

Three things follow, and we would rather you know all three before you take the first picture:

  1. The photo leaves your device. It goes to Google, not to us. We never receive it, store it, or see it.
  2. Your agreement about that photo is with Google, not with us. What Google does with it is governed by the Gemini API terms you accepted when you created the key.
  3. On Google's free tier, Google may use what you send to improve their products, and human reviewers may read it. Google's paid tier does not work this way. Most people using Replicost will be on the free tier, because that is what our setup flow points at.

We will not describe identification with your key as private, confidential, or on-device, because none of those would be true of it.

Location is stripped before the photo is sent. Replicost removes the EXIF and IPTC metadata blocks — which is where GPS coordinates, timestamps, and device identifiers live — from every image before it goes to Google. This happens for photos you take, photos you choose from your library, photos shared into the app from elsewhere, and receipt photos alike.

The model is asked to describe what it sees. It never sets a price. Every number in Replicost comes from our own cost data, computed on your device.

Lookups you start

Each of these sends one request, only when you tap for it, straight from your phone to a public service. We never receive any of it.

  • Your address, to the US Census Bureau (geocoding.geo.census.gov), when you type an address in your shop profile, so the app can find your metro area and state. The address you typed is sent; the app keeps only the metro and state it gets back.
  • A vehicle VIN, to NHTSA (vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov), when you decode a VIN on a car estimate.
  • A vehicle's year, make and model, to NHTSA (api.nhtsa.gov), when you tap the recalls and owner complaints card on a car estimate.
  • "More from Plantburgh" (plantburgh.github.io, hosted by GitHub), when you open that row on the You tab: the app reads a public file listing our other apps, with their icons. The request contains nothing about you; as with any web request, GitHub sees your IP address. The list is kept on your phone for a few hours. Tapping an app opens its store page directly.

Each service handles what it receives under its own policy.

Price updates

Up to twice a day the app checks two public files of current prices (plantburgh.github.io, hosted by GitHub): the same published prices the app already cites, re-read more recently, and the day's gold and silver spot prices. It is how an estimate stays current between app updates. The request contains nothing about you and nothing about what you price; as with any web request, GitHub sees your IP address. The file is kept on your phone so the app works offline.

Your phone's assistant

On Android 16 and later, an assistant you use on your phone (such as Gemini) can ask Replicost what something costs or check a quote for you. Replicost works the answer out on your phone, from the same prices the app uses, and hands it to that assistant. Replicost sends nothing anywhere to do this and keeps no record of the question. What the assistant does with your question and the answer is covered by that assistant's own policy.

The home-screen widget

If you add the Replicost widget to your home screen, it shows your most recently saved estimate. The app writes that one estimate's title and figures, and your theme colours, to a small file on your phone that the widget reads. The file never leaves the phone and is removed with Erase everything.

Currency

You can choose a currency for figures to be shown in. The exchange rates ship inside the app with the date they were read; choosing a currency sends nothing anywhere. The choice is kept on your phone. Replicost's prices are US prices from US sources: a converted figure is the US price in your currency, not a local price.

A contractor's quote

When you check a quote, what you paste or type, including the contractor's figures and hourly rate, is read on your phone and kept only with that job on your phone. It is not sent anywhere.

The welcome screens

The first time you open Replicost it shows three short screens. Whether you have seen them is remembered on your phone and nowhere else.

Plans and purchases

  • Plan settings file. Up to twice a day the app downloads a small public file (plantburgh.github.io, hosted by GitHub) that says what the free plan includes. The request contains nothing about you; as with any web request, GitHub sees your IP address.
  • Google Play Billing. Buying, restoring and checking Replicost Pro happen through Google Play. Google handles your payment details; we never receive them. The app asks Google Play only whether this Google account owns Replicost Pro.
  • The free trial is Google Play's introductory offer on Replicost Pro: free for 14 days, then the monthly price unless you cancel in Google Play before it ends. Google Play decides who is eligible (one per Google account) and runs it; we never see your payment details.
  • The free plan's counts stay on your phone when you use "Erase everything": quote checks used this month and last, extra checks earned by watching an ad, the last day each tool was unlocked by an ad, and the day the app was first opened. So a reinstall does not reset them, they are also kept outside the app. On Android this is Google Play services' Block Store (one small entry, replicost_limits_v1), which belongs to your Google account, outlasts uninstalling the app and comes back on a new phone with that account; on an iPhone it is your iCloud Keychain. It holds dates and counts only, nothing about you or your estimates, and we never receive it.

Crash reports

If the app crashes in a store build, Google Firebase Crashlytics receives a crash report so we can fix it: the error, where in the app it happened, your device model, Android version, app version and a random installation number Crashlytics creates for this install. It never includes your estimates, prices, addresses, photos, quotes, voice or key. Firebase keeps crash reports for 90 days. Uninstalling the app ends them.

Reading a photo on your phone (no key)

Without a Gemini key, Replicost can still name what is in a photo and read a receipt, using Google ML Kit, which runs on your phone. The photo does not leave your device. ML Kit itself may send Google basic diagnostic and usage information (such as the device model, app version and how long a reading took) to keep the feature working; it does not send the photo or the text it reads. Google's ML Kit terms govern that.

Search help on your phone

When something you type does not match a job Replicost prices, the app can try a few more things. Each one runs on your phone or only when you tap it:

  • Picking the closest job. On phones that have it, the phone's own AI model (Google's Gemini Nano, part of Android) is given what you typed and a list of Replicost's own job names, and may pick one. It runs on the phone; your words are not sent to us or to a server by Replicost. The app shows "Read as: …" so you see what was priced.
  • Translation. A question in Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese or Arabic can be translated to English on the phone (Google ML Kit). The first time, the phone downloads that language's translation model from Google; after that it works offline.
  • Voice. Tapping VOICE uses your phone's speech service to turn what you say into text. Depending on the phone, that service may send the audio to Google or Apple to transcribe it, under their terms. Replicost keeps only the text, in the search box.
  • Scan a label. A photo of an appliance's rating plate is read on the phone (ML Kit, as above). A model number can be looked up with a plain web search, only if you tap "Look it up".
  • Use my location. Only when you tap it, the phone's approximate location is used once to pick the nearest city Replicost prices. It is not stored or sent anywhere by us.
  • Searches we could not price. The last 50 are kept on your phone (You › Searches we could not price). They leave your phone only if you tap "Send them to us", which opens your own email app with the list filled in; nothing is sent until you press send there. "Clear the list" removes them, and so does erasing the app's data.

Camera, microphone, photo library and location permissions

The app asks for camera access to photograph a subject, and photo library access to choose an existing picture. Both are used for identification and nothing else. Decline either and the rest of the app works normally — typing what you want priced needs no permissions at all. The microphone is asked for only when you tap VOICE, and approximate location only when you tap "Use my location"; both are used for that one moment and nothing else (see "Search help on your phone").

Deleting your data

You · Erase everything on this device removes every saved estimate, project, ledger, report, quote observation, note, your shop profile, and your stored Gemini key. It cannot be undone.

Because nothing was ever on a server, there is no backup on a server either. The only copy that can survive an erase or a lost device is a backup file you save yourself: You · Back up or restore writes your saved estimates, projects, ledgers, reports, quote observations, and shop profile into one JSON file and hands it to the share sheet. Where it goes next — your email, your cloud drive, a computer — is your choice and under that service's terms, not ours; we never see it. The file never contains your Gemini key. Restoring reads such a file back in, and only from a file you pick.

Two things survive an erase, on purpose:

  • A redeemed access code, if you have one. That is a grant of access rather than data about you, and it cannot be re-issued if destroyed.
  • The free plan's counts and first-open date (see "Plans and purchases"), kept in your Google account's Block Store or your iCloud Keychain so that erasing or reinstalling does not restart them. They are dates and counts only. We cannot see or delete them; they are kept with your Google account's backup for this app (or your iCloud Keychain), and deleting that backup removes them.

There is no Replicost account to delete and no email list to leave. If you wrote to us, ask and we will delete that email thread.

To delete photos you have already sent to Google, use your Google account — we cannot reach them.

Children

Replicost is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect anything from anyone, of any age.

What the store forms say

For clarity, these are the answers we give on Apple's privacy nutrition labels and Google Play's Data Safety form, and they follow from everything above:

  • Data collected by us: crash logs and diagnostics only (see "Crash reports"), to fix crashes; not linked to you, never shared, never used for ads.
  • Data shared with third parties: photographs, to Google, only when you have connected your own API key and only at the moment you identify something. Optional, user-initiated, and never for advertising. An address you type, to the US Census Bureau, and a VIN or a vehicle's year, make and model, to NHTSA, only when you tap for that lookup. Spoken questions, to the phone's speech service (Google or Apple), only when you tap VOICE.
  • Data stored on device: estimates, profile, reports, API key, the last 50 searches the app could not price.
  • Tracking: none by us. In the store version only, Google's Mobile Ads SDK collects the advertising ID and device signals for ads (see below).

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the app will show the new version on next launch. The date at the top always reflects the current text.

Advertising — the store version only

The version of Replicost installed from an app store shows advertising. It is the only version that does; a personal or sideloaded build shows none and makes none of the requests described here.

Ads are served by Google's Mobile Ads SDK. To do that, the SDK collects the device's advertising identifier and device signals (model, operating system, IP address, approximate location derived from it, and interaction with the ad) and sends them to Google under Google's privacy policy. We do not receive this data. Where the law requires it, you are asked for consent before the first ad request, and you can change that choice from the same prompt later.

Ads appear only on the free plan, never during the free trial or with Pro, beside the app's screens and never inside an estimate, an export, or a shared record. Some ads are optional videos you choose to watch to use a Pro tool for a while. Nothing you type, photograph, save, or report is used to target them. A partner card may be picked to suit the kind of job on screen (a tire offer beside a car estimate); that choice is made on your phone from the card's own words, and nothing about the job is sent. Cards for our own apps and partner offers come from our server (app.giganize.work); opening one goes through a link that counts the tap, with nothing about you attached.
