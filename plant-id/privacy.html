Plantburgh Plant ID: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-15 · Plantburgh Products LLC, a Pennsylvania company owned by Plantburgh LLC

The short version: To name or diagnose a plant, the photo you scan goes to our server. Hidden photo data such as GPS location is removed first. Our own plant model checks it, and our AI provider may too. Your garden, reminders and scan history are kept on our servers under your account. We do not sell your data. The free plan shows ads.

Who we are

Plantburgh Plant ID is made by Plantburgh Products LLC, a Pennsylvania company owned by Plantburgh LLC. Questions about privacy: contact@plantburgh.com.

Your account

The app signs you in as a guest tied to this phone, using Google Firebase. No name or email is needed. You can choose to link a Google account so your garden follows you to a new phone; then we receive your Google account's email address and name from Google. Without a linked Google account, uninstalling the app or clearing its data starts a new guest, and the old account can no longer be reached from the phone. Settings › About shows your account code.

Photos you scan

  • Each photo you scan (identify, diagnose, repot check, expert question, plant timeline) is sent to our server on Google Cloud in the United States.
  • Before anything stores or reads it, our server removes hidden data inside the photo, such as GPS location and camera details.
  • Our own plant-recognition model, which runs on our Google Cloud servers, compares the photo with known plants. When it is not sure, the photo also goes to our AI provider, Anthropic, to produce the answer. Anthropic processes it under its commercial terms, which do not allow using it to train their models.
  • If a photo shows a plant label (a garden sign or nursery tag), the app reads the text on your phone. Only the plant's name is sent to our server, together with the photo for your history.
  • We keep your scans in your history. The photo of a scan you never saved to your garden is deleted after one year; the plant's name and date stay in your history. Photo links in the app expire after one day.

What we store

Your garden (plants, folders, notes, photos, care settings), reminders, wish list, scan and diagnosis history, Plant Advisor conversations, expert questions and our answers, your plan, and usage counts (scans, pages and AI cost per day and month) that run the plan limits. It is stored in Google Firebase in the United States under your account.

Your location

The app never reads your phone's GPS. You can choose your city in the app. We use it, rounded to about 11 km, to show weather warnings from the US National Weather Service and the plants that are native or invasive where you live.

Plant Advisor and expert questions

Plant Advisor messages are answered by our AI provider. Expert questions, with any photos you add, are read and answered by people at Plantburgh. Please do not include personal details you do not want us to see.

Ads on the free plan

The free plan shows a Google AdMob banner, or one of our own products or a partner offer when AdMob has nothing to show. You can choose to watch a rewarded ad to earn an extra scan; Google then tells our server the ad was watched. Google may use your phone's advertising ID, IP address and device details to show and measure ads under Google's policies, and people in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland are asked for consent first. Your photos, garden and questions are never sent to advertisers. Premium shows no ads.

Free trial and purchases

Premium is billed by Google Play; Google tells our server which plan your account has, and we never see your payment details. The free trial is started in the app, needs no card and is never billed. To give one trial per phone, the app sends a one-way code made from your phone's Android ID (the ID itself never leaves the phone). We keep that code after you delete your account, so the trial cannot be repeated.

Keeping the service safe

To stop abuse we count requests per network address. We store only a one-way code made from the address, never the address itself, and delete it after three days. Google Firebase App Check helps confirm that requests come from the real app.

Notifications

Care reminders and the notice before your free trial ends are scheduled on your phone. When you tap Done or Later on a reminder, in the app or on the home-screen widget, that tap is saved to your account so your care history stays up to date.

Plant photos and plant names

The reference photos of plants and plant problems are free-licence pictures from Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons; each shows its author and licence. Plant names and their earlier names come from the World Checklist of Vascular Plants by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew (CC BY). The plants a species is often mixed up with are informed by the North Carolina Extension Gardener Plant Toolbox. These sources receive nothing about you.

Sharing

We do not sell or rent your data. We share it only with the services that run the app (Google Firebase and Cloud, Anthropic, Google AdMob, Google Play, the US National Weather Service for your city's forecast, Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons for public plant photos, and GBIF.org, which receives only a plant's Latin name to draw its world map), or when the law requires it.

Keeping and deleting

We keep your data until you delete your account. Delete it any time in the app (Settings › Delete account): everything stored under your account is erased right away. You can also ask by email; see Delete your data. Deleting your account does not cancel a Google Play subscription.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data, a correction, or deletion at contact@plantburgh.com. Premium members can also export their garden as a spreadsheet in the app. We answer within 30 days.

Children

Plantburgh Plant ID is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If we change this policy, we update the date at the top of this page.
