Plantburgh Plant ID: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-21 · Plantburgh Products LLC, a Pennsylvania company owned by Plantburgh LLC

The short version: To name or diagnose a plant, the photo you scan goes to our server. Hidden photo data such as GPS location is removed first. Our own plant model checks it, and our AI provider may too. Your garden, reminders and scan history are kept on our servers under your account. We do not sell your data. The free plan shows ads. An AI assistant sees your garden or your plant photos only if you connect one and switch that on.

Who we are

Plantburgh Plant ID is made by Plantburgh Products LLC, a Pennsylvania company owned by Plantburgh LLC. Questions about privacy: contact@plantburgh.com.

Your account

The app signs you in as a guest tied to this phone, using Google Firebase. No name or email is needed. You can choose to link a Google account so your garden follows you to a new phone; then we receive your Google account's email address and name from Google. Without a linked Google account, uninstalling the app or clearing its data starts a new guest, and the old account can no longer be reached from the phone. Settings › About shows your account code.

Photos you scan

  • Each photo you scan (identify, diagnose, repot check, expert question, plant timeline) is sent to our server on Google Cloud in the United States.
  • Before anything stores or reads it, our server removes hidden data inside the photo, such as GPS location and camera details.
  • Our own plant-recognition model, which runs on our Google Cloud servers, compares the photo with known plants. When it is not sure, the photo also goes to our AI provider, Anthropic, to produce the answer. Anthropic processes it under its commercial terms, which do not allow using it to train their models.
  • If a photo shows a plant label (a garden sign or nursery tag), the app reads the text on your phone. Only the plant's name is sent to our server, together with the photo for your history.
  • We keep your scans in your history. The photo of a scan you never saved to your garden is deleted after one year; the plant's name and date stay in your history. Photo links in the app expire after one day.

What we store

Your garden (plants, folders, notes, photos, care settings), reminders, wish list, scan and diagnosis history, Plant Advisor conversations, expert questions and our answers, notes you send us about a plant page, your plan, the day you last opened the app, and usage counts (scans, pages and AI cost per day and month) that run the plan limits. It is stored in Google Firebase in the United States under your account.

On your phone: the app keeps the plant pages you open (the 300 most recent) so they open again without a signal, and it carries short care notes for every plant we have a page for. These are plant pages only, with nothing about you in them. The saved pages are wiped when you sign out or delete your account.

Your location

The app never reads your phone's GPS. You can choose your town in the app: towns in the US and its territories, the United Kingdom and Australia are listed, and anywhere else you can type a city and country. To show plants that grow near you, the app sends our server your state or region and country (or the city you typed, for a home outside those countries); your exact town stays on your phone. Your home decides which native and invasive list you see: only the list for where you live, so a plant native at home is never labelled invasive because of another country's list. For US homes, the app sends your town's position, rounded to about 11 km, to the US National Weather Service for weather warnings; there is no weather outside the US yet. A home south of the equator gets seasons six months apart (watering, feeding, what is in bloom); for your reminders the app tells our server only whether your home is south of the equator, yes or no.

Plant Advisor and expert questions

Plant Advisor messages are answered by our AI provider. Expert questions, with any photos you add, are read and answered by people at Plantburgh. Our AI provider reads each question first: it checks that the photo is clear enough to use, answers a simple question right away, and otherwise prepares a draft that our expert reviews and rewrites. If you asked in another language, it also translates our answer for you. Please do not include personal details you do not want us to see.

Ads on the free plan

The free plan shows a Google AdMob banner, or one of our own products or a partner offer when AdMob has nothing to show. You can choose to watch a rewarded ad to earn one extra scan a day; Google then tells our server the ad was watched. To keep it to one a day per phone, the app sends the same one-way code made from your phone's Android ID with the ad, and our server keeps that code with the day for three days, then deletes it. Google may use your phone's advertising ID, IP address and device details to show and measure ads under Google's policies, and people in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland are asked for consent first. Your photos, garden and questions are never sent to advertisers. Premium shows no ads.

Free trial and purchases

Premium is billed by Google Play; Google tells our server which plan your account has, and we never see your payment details. The free trial is started in the app, needs no card and is never billed. To give one trial per phone, the app sends a one-way code made from your phone's Android ID (the ID itself never leaves the phone). We keep that code after you delete your account, so the trial cannot be repeated.

Keeping the service safe

To stop abuse we count requests per network address. We store only a one-way code made from the address, never the address itself, and delete it after three days. Google Firebase App Check helps confirm that requests come from the real app.

Notifications

Care reminders and the notice before your free trial ends are scheduled on your phone. When you tap Done or Later on a reminder, in the app or on the home-screen widget, that tap is saved to your account so your care history stays up to date.

When you send an expert question, the app asks if it may notify you when your answer arrives. If you allow it, we store your phone's notification address (a code from Google Firebase Cloud Messaging) under your account and use it only to send that notice. We keep at most five phones per account, drop an address when it stops working, and erase it when you delete your account. You can turn notifications off any time in your phone's settings.

Plant photos and plant names

The reference photos of plants and plant problems are free-licence pictures from Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons; each shows its author and licence. Plant names and their earlier names come from the World Checklist of Vascular Plants by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew (CC BY). The plants a species is often mixed up with are informed by the North Carolina Extension Gardener Plant Toolbox. More photos of a plant come from iNaturalist: open-licence photos only, each shown with its author and licence; our server sends iNaturalist only the plant's Latin name. Whether a plant lives outdoors where you are is worked out on your phone from the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map (USDA Agricultural Research Service and the PRISM Climate Group at Oregon State University), which is stored in the app. Whether a plant is native or introduced where you live comes from the same Kew checklist, area by area. The town lists come from the US Census Bureau (US states and Puerto Rico) and GeoNames (CC BY 4.0: the US Virgin Islands, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, the United Kingdom and Australia). Invasive plant lists come from each US state's own lists; in the UK from the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 and the Wildlife (Northern Ireland) Order 1985 (Schedule 9) and the UK list of invasive alien species of special concern, which contain public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0; and in Australia from the Weeds of National Significance (Australian Government, CC BY 4.0). These sources receive nothing about you.

If you correct a plant's name, we count that correction without your account ID, so wrong answers can be found and fixed. You can remove any scan and its photo from your history.

Sharing your plant photos is off unless you turn it on in Settings. If you turn it on, a copy of the photo of a plant you save to My Garden can be shown to other people in the app after we have looked at it, with the name you typed or as "A Plantburgh Plant ID user". Nothing else about you is shown. Turning sharing off, or deleting your account, removes every photo you shared.

AI assistants you connect

The address for everyone. AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini can ask our server for short plant facts (name, light, watering, hardiness zones, toxicity), reference photos with their credits, common plant problems and a short description of our other apps. It is read-only, needs no account, and has nothing to do with your garden, your photos or your account. We keep only a short-lived count per internet address, stored as a one-way code, to prevent overuse.

Your personal address. In Settings › Connect an assistant you can make a personal address and paste it into an assistant you use. This is your choice; nothing is connected until you do it. We store a one-way fingerprint of the address (never the address itself), when an assistant last used it, the name that assistant gives itself, and how many times it asked that day. The address is kept on the phone that made it.

A personal address has two switches, both off until you turn them on:

  • Let it read my garden: the assistant can read your saved plants: their names, where each one lives, its pot, its care plan, what care is due, your recent care and your notes.
  • Let it see my plant photos: the assistant can look at the newest photos you took of a saved plant, to see how it is doing and give advice.

An assistant can only read. It can never change or delete anything in your account. What it reads is sent to the company that runs that assistant (for example OpenAI, Anthropic or Google) and is then handled under that company's own privacy terms, which we do not control. Keep your personal address private: anyone who has it can read what you switched on. Turning a switch off, tapping Turn off, making a new address or deleting your account stops access right away; it cannot take back what an assistant has already received.

Sharing

We do not sell or rent your data. We share it only with the services that run the app (Google Firebase and Cloud, Anthropic, Google AdMob, Google Play, the US National Weather Service for a US town's forecast, Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons for public plant photos, and GBIF.org, which receives only a plant's Latin name to draw its world map), with an AI assistant you connect yourself, which receives only what you switched on, or when the law requires it.

Keeping and deleting

We keep your data until you delete your account. Delete it any time in the app (Settings › Delete account): everything stored under your account is erased right away. You can also ask by email; see Delete your data. Deleting your account does not cancel a Google Play subscription.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data, a correction, or deletion at contact@plantburgh.com. Premium members can also export their garden as a spreadsheet in the app. We answer within 30 days.

If you live in the United Kingdom or Australia

Where your data is kept. Our servers are in the United States (Google Firebase and Cloud). If you use the app from the UK or Australia, your data is sent to and kept in the United States.

Why we use it (UK). We use your data to give you the app you asked for (your garden, scans, reminders and plan), to keep the service safe from misuse (our legitimate interest), and, for personalised ads, only with your consent. People in the UK are asked about ads by Google's consent form before any ad loads, and can change their choice any time in Settings › Ad choices.

Your rights. In the UK you can ask to see, correct, delete or move your data, object to how we use it, or ask us to limit it, and you can withdraw consent at any time. If you are unhappy with our answer you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk). In Australia you can ask to see or correct your data, and complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au). Write to contact@plantburgh.com first and we will try to put it right.

Children

Plantburgh Plant ID is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data.
