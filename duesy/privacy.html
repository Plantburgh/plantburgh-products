Duesy Privacy Policy

Effective 2026-10-16 · Plantburgh Products LLC, a Pennsylvania company owned by Plantburgh LLC

Español

The short version: we collect nothing about your ledger. There is no account, and your ledger never reaches a server of ours (the only thing Duesy fetches from us is the list of banners). Everything you enter stays on your phone, and the only copy that ever leaves it is encrypted with a key we never have. We (the developer) cannot see your ledger, your contacts, your calls, or your phone number. The free version shows ads from Google AdMob, described below; Duesy Pro shows none.

Data stored on your device

Your ledger — people, debts, settlements, notes, limits, phone numbers, photos and receipts you attach — lives in a local database encrypted at rest. The encryption key is kept in your device's secure hardware. You can additionally require fingerprint / face unlock to open the app.

Contacts (optional)

If you grant contacts access, Duesy reads names, phone numbers, and photos only to copy the ones you pick into your local ledger. Access is read-only — Duesy never modifies a contact — and nothing is uploaded or matched against any external service. Revoking the permission or deleting a contact leaves your ledger intact.

Incoming calls (optional)

If you make Duesy your call-screening app, the operating system shows it each incoming number so it can display your own note about that caller — the standing you recorded. Matching happens entirely on the device. Duesy never blocks, filters, or rates calls, keeps no general call log, and never transmits a number anywhere. A short in-app list of unrecognised callers (so you can link a new number to someone) is capped, expires on its own, and can be turned off.

After a call (optional)

With phone-state permission, Android tells Duesy only that a call started and ended — never the number — so it can offer a "did they pay?" reminder after a real conversation. This stays on the device and can be declined; the feature then falls back to a simple timer or nothing.

Lock-screen privacy

By default a call notification shows only a name on the locked screen; amounts appear after you unlock. You can show more, or nothing but the app name, in Settings.

Payment apps and shared receipts

Pay buttons open Venmo / Cash App with details prefilled — those apps operate under their own policies; Duesy sends them nothing beyond the link you tap. If you share a payment confirmation into Duesy, it is parsed on the device and saved only if you confirm it.

Automatic backup

Unless you turn it off, Duesy saves an encrypted copy of your ledger (and photos you attached) into the part of the app that Android's own backup carries to your Google account. The copy is AES-256 encrypted with a random key that Duesy stores in Google Block Store, and only when Block Store is end-to-end encrypted (your phone has a screen lock). Neither Google nor we can read it; on a new phone set up with the same Google account, Duesy can open it and offer it back. On iPhone the key uses iCloud Keychain. Nothing else in the app is included in device backups.

Ads (free version only)

If you use Duesy without Pro after the trial, a small banner appears on the home screen and on a person's tab. Some banners come from Google AdMob, which may collect your device's advertising ID, basic device information, and ad interactions, and may personalise ads where you consent (Google's consent form appears the first time). See how Google uses data in ads. Other banners are our own: links to Plantburgh services and apps, and affiliate offers marked "Sponsored", which may earn us a commission if you buy. To show those, Duesy downloads a public list of banners from our server every few hours; that request carries nothing about you. When you tap one of our banners, the link passes through our server, which adds one to that banner's daily tap count (no IP address or device id is kept) and sends you on. Ads never see your ledger, names, amounts, contacts or calls. Pro, trial and the ad-free state make no ad requests at all.

Assistants and links (off unless you turn them on)

In Settings you can turn on "Let assistants read my balances". While it is on, Duesy keeps a short summary of who owes whom in its private storage on your phone, and assistants you have allowed on that phone (such as Gemini on Android) can read it when you ask them. Duesy never sends it anywhere and deletes it when you turn the setting off. Assistants, other apps and duesy:// links can only open Duesy on a filled-in screen: nothing is added, settled, deleted or sent until you tap it. If you add your Cash App, Venmo or PayPal.me name under "Your pay links", it stays on your phone and goes only into reminder texts you choose to send.

Importing

If you import a Splitwise export or a spreadsheet, the file is read on your phone and only the rows you confirm are saved. It is never uploaded.

Backups and sharing with friends

You can also export a backup yourself; it is encrypted with a passphrase only you know and goes wherever you choose to share it. Sync updates and signed statements travel directly from you to your friend through apps you pick (Messages, QR, etc.); no server of ours is involved, and we never receive a copy.

Purchases

Subscriptions are processed by Google Play or the Apple App Store under their own privacy policies. Duesy only learns whether a subscription is active.

Data retention and deletion

We retain nothing, so there is nothing for us to delete. To erase all app data, delete the app (or clear its storage). The automatic backup lives in your Google account's device backup, which you can delete in your Google account settings; turning automatic backup off in Duesy stops new copies. Backups you exported are yours to delete wherever you saved them. AdMob data is governed by Google's policies and your Android ad settings (Settings, Google, Ads).

Children

Duesy is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version is posted at this address with a new effective date, and the app tells you what changed after an update.
